2. What is Gynecomastia?

Gynecomastia is the enlargement of breast tissue in boys and men. It means 'Man having breasts like a woman' and is derived from the Latin words 'Gynecos' - meaning woman' and 'Mastos' meaning Breast. The term Gynecomastia was coined by Galen in 2nd century AD.

4. What are the common names used to denote Gynecomastia?

Gynecomastia is often called as Male breasts or Man boobs.

5. What are the common causes of Gynecomastia?
  • Pubertal Gynecomastia – occurs during puberty or teenage
  • Senile Gynecomastia (Between 50-80 years age).
  • Obesity
  • Malnutrition
  • Liver Failure
  • Kidney Failure
  • Thyrotoxicosis
  • Hormone producing tumours of testis, pituitary, adrenal glands.
  • Steroids
  • Certain medications like ketoconozole, spironolactone,finesteride,opioids, omeprazole, anti-retroviral drugs etc.
  • Drugs like marijuana, heroin, amphetamine, methadone.
6. What are the different types of Gynecomastia?

7. What are the different grades of Gynecomastia?

The American Society of Plastic Surgery (ASPS) - descriptive classification called the "gynecomastia scale". Gynecomastia is classified into four groups according to the severity of the breast tissue enlargement.

The four grades are as follows:

Grade I

Grade I gynecomastia is defined as small breast enlargement with a localized button of tissue around the areola or the surrounding nipple area.

Grade II

Grade II gynecomastia defined as mild to moderate breast enlargement, which expands up to the width of the chest. The tissue is now broader based on the chest.

Grade III

Grade III gynecomastia is defined as moderate to significant breast enlargement, expanding the chest's width with excess skin. Although the swelling of the breast tissue is considered moderate to severe, this grade is highly

Grade IV

Grade IV gynecomastia is a significant breast enlargement with substantial excess skin. This grade is considered severe and highly visible, giving the person a feminine breast appearance.

8. How is True gynecomastia diagnosed?

True Gynecomastia feels like a small, rubbery lump under the nipple(s) and areola. It can happen on one or both sides and the lump may feel sore. To diagnose gynecomastia, a thorough history is obtained and a physical examination is performed by the treating surgeon.

9. What is Pseudo-gynecomastia?

Pseudo-gynecomastia is a condition where the male breast tissue enlargement is solely due to excess fatty tissue without any breast tissue. This condition is treated by liposuction alone.

10. Is Gynecomastia dangerous to health?

Physiologic or Idiopathic gynecomastia is not dangerous to health. It can be corrected by surgery if the person desires to have a good chest shape and the condition persists for more than 2 years.

Pathologic or Non-Physiologic Gynecomastia requires a thorough evaluation to detect the underlying cause and treat it.

11. Is it possible to correct Gynaecomastia by exercise, weight loss or diet?

No, it is not possibleto correct Gynaecomastia by exercise, weight loss or diet.

14. When can one join office after Gynecomastia?

One can join office 2 days after the surgery and can perform light activities without straining.

15. What can one expect post-surgery?
  • Minimal pain and discomfort for 3-5 days.
  • Can join office after 2 days rest.
  • Restricted shoulder movements and only light activities for 10-14 days.
  • The improvement in shape is visible immediately, but as the swelling subsides and the skin reshapes, the chest shapes appears better. One can expect to see the final results by 6-8 weeks.

16. How long should one wear the compression garment?

Compression vest or garment is an elastic garment which helps by controlling the swelling, providing support and facilitating skin retraction. The vest should be worn continuously for the first 4-6 weeks after surgery to achieve the most benefit.

17. When can one expect the results?

By 10-14 days, noticeable change can be seen in the chest shape. One can expect to see the final results by 6-8 weeks.

18. What are the common complications?

The most frequent early complication following surgical management of gynecomastia is hematoma. Seroma, over-resection with saucer-type deformity, under-resection, unappealing scarring and infections are also possible but rare complications.

When performed by a skilful and experienced surgeon and with good post-surgery care, the chances of the complications are very low.
